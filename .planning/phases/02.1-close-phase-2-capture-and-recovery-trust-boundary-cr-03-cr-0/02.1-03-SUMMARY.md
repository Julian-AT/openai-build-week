## Decisions Made

- The source identity owns one publication root, while canonical inventory SHA-256 owns immutable generation identity. Renderer/provider indices and sequential recovery filenames never participate.
- Inventory closes over the archive, accepted journal, optional invalid suffix, quarantine metadata, byte counts, roles, and hashes. Archive and quarantine cannot become independently visible.
- Generation publication uses exclusive rename plus parent fsync. Visibility uses an exclusively installed canonical pointer, followed by pointer fsync and source-root fsync.
- A losing identical publisher may complete an installed pointer's interrupted durability only after verifying the winner; it revalidates after fsync before returning reuse. A different winner remains immutable and yields `publication_conflict`.
- Recovery ignores unreferenced generations and issues `VerifiedArchive`/`RecoveredArchive` only after complete pointer, source, inventory, member, and semantic revalidation.

## Threat Model Closure

- **T-02.1-05 (generation/member tampering): mitigated.** Every member is hash/count/role bound by the canonical inventory and both staged and final generations pass complete reread plus `ArchiveVerifier` before visibility.
- **T-02.1-06 (concurrent cleanup denial): mitigated.** First-writer exclusive arbitration preserves complete candidates, while deferred cleanup is constrained to the invocation's unique staging token.
- **T-02.1-07 (pointer/source spoofing): mitigated.** Pointer format, source identity, generation ID, inventory digest, session, manifest, accepted prefix, and every member are verified before capability issuance.
- **T-02.1-08 (crash-outcome repudiation): mitigated.** Deterministic operation traces and restart assertions cover every before/after durability edge plus both publisher arrival orders.
